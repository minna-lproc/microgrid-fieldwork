How to use this instrument
The survey has two instruments. Use Instrument 1 (Parts A to H) with a sample of households and enterprises. Use Instrument 2 (Parts I to L) with barangay officials, facility heads and community leaders as key informants, one per community. Together they collect the inputs that microgrid design normally needs: a load profile, reliability and resilience expectations, willingness and ability to pay, the energy resources and land available, and the community’s acceptance of and role in the project.

Notes for the Project Team
Why each part is there
Part
Design input it provides
A, B
Baseline of customers, existing supply and current energy spending. Gives the fuel and cost savings against which a microgrid is judged.
C, D
Load profile by hour and season, plus expected growth, for sizing generation and storage. Sizing depends on both geographic conditions and load demand.
E, J
Reliability and resilience targets, critical loads and hazards. The planning stage asks what level of uptime is needed, which operations must stay on, and which can tolerate instability.
F, G
Affordability, payment mode and customer-service design. Tariff, subsidy and service standards for unserved and underserved areas are determined through the regulatory process.
H, L
Social acceptance, governance, FPIC and local operation and maintenance capacity. These matter for the operator and for community consent.
I, K
Community size, layout, access, land and natural resources, used for siting and network layout.
